## Summary

This study presents the first integrated metabolomics-proteomics analysis of ethylene-induced isoflavonoid biosynthesis in soybean, revealing a novel metabolic priming mechanism characterized by differential accumulation of conjugated versus basal metabolites.

## Key Findings

1. **Pathway-level activation**: Ethylene treatment significantly induces secondary metabolite biosynthesis (KEGG map01110, P=0.030), with isoflavonoid biosynthesis as the primary activated pathway.

2. **Novel discovery - Conjugate-selective accumulation**: We demonstrate for the first time that malonylated and acetylated isoflavonoid conjugates accumulate massively (>4,000-fold increase), while basal aglycones show minimal changes (~1.1-fold). This differential pattern reveals tight coupling between biosynthesis and conjugation, representing a sophisticated metabolic priming strategy.

3. **Multi-omics concordance**: All six key biosynthetic enzymes (IFS, IFR, CHI, CHS, PAL, 4CL) show significant upregulation (2.9-6.4-fold, all P<0.05), with 100% directional concordance with metabolite changes, demonstrating coordinated multi-level pathway regulation.

4. **Cross-database validation**: Both KEGG and PlantCyc databases independently identify isoflavonoid/secondary metabolism as top-ranked pathways, providing robust biological validation.

## Significance and Novelty

This work advances our understanding of hormone-mediated metabolic regulation in several important ways:

- **Mechanistic insight**: The dramatic disparity between conjugate and aglycone accumulation provides new understanding of metabolic flux control and defense priming in plants.

- **Agricultural relevance**: Isoflavonoids are key defense compounds and health-promoting nutrients in soybean. Understanding their regulation has implications for crop improvement and biofortification.

- **Methodological contribution**: Our integrated multi-omics approach with cross-database validation provides a robust framework for pathway analysis in plant specialized metabolism.

- **Database development**: We systematically assess KEGG mapping coverage (36.7%), highlighting the need for expanded annotation of plant-specialized conjugates.
